Render a programme to 5.1, 7.1 or 7.1.4 and write it as DTS-HD Master Audio — for 7.1.4, with the DTS:X extension's standard profile.

Takes a master set or an ADM BW64 file at 48 kHz. Its bed channels are routed to their speakers and its objects panned on the room's cube, as `iamf` renders them. The render is coded losslessly beside a 5.1 core; for 7.1.4 each height is folded into the corner below it at −3 dB, the 7.1 that makes coded losslessly at 24 bits, and the heights carried losslessly in the extension, and a limiter keeps every corner with its height in it under full scale. Writes a raw elementary stream, or a Matroska file whose one track is the stream (`A_DTS`) when `--out` ends in `.mka` or `.mkv`. See `docs/dts.md`.

Usage: harlettizer.exe dts [OPTIONS] --out <OUT> <INPUT>

Arguments:
  <INPUT>
          A master set's `.atmos` config, or an ADM BW64 file

Options:
      --config <PATH>
          Local settings file, for what is this machine's rather than the programme's: the key Evolution frames are signed with.
          
          Defaults to `$HARLETTIZER_CONFIG`, then `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/harlettizer/config.yaml`, then `~/.config/harlettizer/config.yaml`; a default that is absent is no settings, a path given here has to exist.

      --out <OUT>
          Write the stream here: into Matroska if it ends in `.mka` or `.mkv`, as a raw elementary stream otherwise

      --frames <FRAMES>
          Stop after this many frames of audio

      --mono-prefix <PREFIX>
          Read the master's audio from one mono WAV per waveform; see `encode --mono-prefix`

      --layout <LAYOUT>
          `7.1.4`, the DTS:X standard profile; `7.1`; or `5.1`
          
          [default: 7.1.4]

      --bits <BITS>
          Bits of the lossless layer: 24, or 16 for 5.1 and 7.1. A 7.1.4 is 24 bits: a height's share of a corner is a 24-bit quantity
          
          [default: 24]

      --adpcm
          Code the core's subbands with prediction (ADPCM). The lossless layers are the same either way

      --progress
          Report progress on standard error

  -h, --help
          Print help (see a summary with '-h')
